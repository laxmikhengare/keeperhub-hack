The obstruction

Migrating isn't repointing a config.
It's permission surgery — and the order can kill you.

Your keeper's address is written into your contracts. Roles, allowlists, onlyKeeper. The replacement has a different address, and the old key can't be exported. So every contract that trusted the dead one has to be found and rewritten, in the right sequence.

Revoke too early

The protocol sits unattended. That's the window where a liquidation is missed and real money leaves.

Revoke too late

A dead credential keeps production write access to a live protocol.

OpenZeppelin“Private keys cannot be exported from Defender's AWS KMS. Every migration requires generating new signer addresses and updating all on-chain permissions.”

Analyst — comprehension

A role is a keccak hash with no registry. It reads the contract source, finds the string literal, and re-hashes every name in code before accepting it. It cannot invent one.

Adjudicator — judgment

The rebuilt keeper runs observe-only against live chain state. This decides whether the disagreements are noise or a regression.

Cutover — proof by doing

Grant, then make the replacement settle three real epochs on chain, then revoke. Confirmed by re-reading the contract, not by trusting the report.

Why it matters

Every dead network leaves protocols holding permissions they can't safely move. That backlog is real, dated, and growing.

Why KeeperHub

Migration is their growth channel — and their own guide still says by hand. This is the tool that motion is missing.

Why an agent

Resolving a role means reading source nobody indexed. Clearing a cutover means judging whether a miss was noise. Neither is a rule.
